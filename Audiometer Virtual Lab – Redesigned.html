Experiment overview

🎯 Aim

To measure pure tone air conduction and study bone conduction thresholds of hearing using an audiometer.

🧰 Equipment required

Audiometer – 1 · Headphones – 1. A bone vibrator is used for the bone conduction demonstration.

Learning objectives

Understand conduction pathways, adjust frequency and intensity, estimate hearing thresholds, and plot an audiogram.

Test range

Air conduction: 250–8000 Hz. Bone conduction: up to 4000 Hz in this experiment.

02 / Background

Theory of audiometry

An audiometer measures hearing sensitivity by presenting tones at different frequencies and intensities. The threshold is the lowest sound level at which the subject can hear the tone.

Air conduction (AC)

Sound travels through the external ear and middle ear to the inner ear. Headphones deliver the test tone.

Bone conduction (BC)

Mechanical vibrations of the skull bones and soft tissues transmit sound to the inner ear. A bone vibrator is used.

How to operate

Testing sequence

  1. Choose the conduction mode and ear.
  2. Select a frequency and set the sound intensity.
  3. Press “Present tone” to start the test.
  4. Check the simulated patient's response.
  5. Adjust intensity and repeat until threshold is reached.
  6. Save the threshold and continue to the next frequency.
Educational simulation only. It is not a calibrated clinical audiometer and cannot diagnose hearing loss.

06 / Lab record

Procedure

  1. Connect the headphones to the audiometer and switch on the instrument.
  2. Select RIGHT or LEFT depending upon the ear to be tested.
  3. Adjust the frequency dial to the required test frequency.
  4. Present a tone at an audible level, then gradually reduce its intensity.
  5. Identify and record the lowest level at which the subject hears the tone.
  6. Repeat the test at different frequencies and verify the response.
  7. For bone conduction, connect the bone vibrator and select BC. Test up to 4000 Hz.
  8. Plot the recorded thresholds on the audiogram.
